Forecasting Next-Day Hourly Electricity Demand in ERCOT

A collaborative forecasting study evaluating whether historical electricity demand, calendar patterns, and selected weather variables can predict ERCOT's next-day hourly electricity consumption.

Collaborative team project · Group 1
Electricity demand forecastingTime series modeling · Model comparison · Peak-demand analysis

Research question

Can historical demand, calendar patterns, and selected weather variables forecast ERCOT's next-day hourly electricity consumption with accuracy approaching the operator's published forecast?

The target was realized hourly electricity demand, measured in megawatt-hours (MWh). The study compared team-developed forecasts with a seasonal naïve baseline and ERCOT's published day-ahead forecast.

Data source

U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Form 930 hourly electricity data.

Forecast target

ERCOT observed hourly electricity demand, recorded in MWh.

Data preparation and feature engineering

The team assembled and validated hourly demand records, checked timestamps and demand values, and prepared predictors representing recurring consumption patterns.

Calendar features

Hour of day, weekday, season, and holiday status, with local calendar features based on Central time.

Historical demand features

24-hour and 168-hour demand lags, plus a historical moving average.

Weather-enhanced features

Regional temperature summaries and heating/cooling indicators derived from selected Texas locations.

Validation considerations

UTC timestamps were retained to avoid daylight-saving ambiguities. Missing benchmark forecasts were not artificially reconstructed.

Forecasting approaches

The project compared a baseline, several statistical and machine-learning approaches, weather-enhanced variants, and a hybrid model.

Modeling approach

An initial full-year SARIMA projection drifted implausibly, so the team shifted to rolling 24-hour forecasts that were updated with observed demand between prediction blocks. The hybrid averaged rolling SARIMA and weather-enhanced XGBoost predictions.

Results and model comparison

The hybrid had the lowest overall error among the team's models. ERCOT's published forecast remained more accurate on the shared test sample.

What the results revealed

Overall accuracy did not tell the whole story. During the highest-demand hours, rolling SARIMA and weather SARIMAX performed much better than the hybrid. The hybrid's MAE in those hours was approximately 3,189 MWh. The report also notes that the XGBoost models under-forecast every one of those peak hours.

Limitations and next steps

  • The comparison used a common set of test timestamps but did not establish identical forecast-issuance times, horizons, or predictor availability across every model.
  • Observed temperatures are not equivalent to weather forecasts available when a real day-ahead prediction is issued.
  • Rapid demand growth challenged models during record peak hours, particularly the tree-based models.
  • Future evaluation should use advance-available weather forecasts, fixed forecast-issuance times, equal 24-hour horizons, and methods that account for demand growth.

The project concluded that model selection should consider both overall accuracy and performance during high-demand conditions. Forecasts should support—not replace—operator judgment.
