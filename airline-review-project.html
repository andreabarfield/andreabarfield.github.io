Airline Review Analysis Using Natural Language Processing

Identifying passenger sentiment and recurring themes in negative airline reviews using text preparation, sentiment analysis, and unsupervised topic clustering.

01 / Project overview

Project objectives and analytical approach

The project organizes unstructured passenger reviews into interpretable patterns. The analysis combines two review datasets, examines sentiment, and groups negative reviews into recurring complaint themes.

01 · PREPARE THE CORPUS

Clean and standardize review data

Combined two review datasets, standardized fields, removed records without review text and duplicate reviews, converted review dates, and normalized the text.

02 · REPRESENT THE LANGUAGE

Convert text into numerical features

Applied Bag of Words, bigrams, and TF-IDF to represent review text for analysis.

03 · EXAMINE SENTIMENT

Identify negative reviews

Used VADER, a lexicon- and rules-based approach, to classify sentiment and focus complaint analysis on negative reviews.

04 · DISCOVER COMPLAINT THEMES

Cluster recurring topics

Applied MiniBatch K-Means to group negative reviews, then interpreted cluster terms and example reviews and evaluated similarity.

07 / Conclusion and recommendations

From passenger language to actionable insight

The project prepared a combined review corpus, represented text numerically, identified sentiment patterns, and organized negative reviews into five interpretable themes. The findings show how NLP can make large volumes of unstructured feedback easier to explore. They describe this review corpus—not all passengers or all airline experiences.

08 / Ethical considerations

Responsible interpretation of review data

Publicly available review text still requires careful consideration of representation, meaning, provenance, privacy, and downstream use. The project materials do not establish the original datasets' collection procedures, consent terms, or privacy protections. Cluster labels are interpretations of word patterns—not verified complaint categories—and results should not be used to rank airlines without accounting for selection bias, review volume, and context.

Key limitation: The corpus represents people who chose to submit reviews to the two source datasets. It does not necessarily represent every passenger, airline, language, or travel experience. Model outputs should support further investigation, not replace contextual judgment.
